Why I Joined the American Express x Resy Restaurant Academy's Inaugural Cohort

By Angela M. Wright, Founder, Wright Hospitality

Nearly 1,000 restaurant operators applied. Thirty were selected. I’m proud that Wright Hospitality is represented in the inaugural American Express x Resy Restaurant Academy cohort.

The six-month program, presented in partnership with the National Restaurant Association Educational Foundation, kicked off in Washington, D.C., with networking and educational sessions. I returned to Detroit energized and ready to put what I learned to work.

For me, this opportunity is about more than adding another credential. It’s about continuing to grow as a leader and finding new ways to strengthen the four hospitality concepts I’ve built: MIX Bricktown, The LOCKEROOM Sports Lounge, Sandbox and Me & You.

What is the American Express x Resy Restaurant Academy?

The American Express x Resy Restaurant Academy is a national leadership development program for independent restaurant owners and managers. The inaugural cohort runs from September 2026 through March 2027 and combines facilitated learning, peer networking and hospitality certifications.

Participants also have the opportunity to learn from accomplished industry professionals, including James Beard Award winners Mashama Bailey, Gregory Gourdet and Nok Suntaranon, along with sommelier and restaurateur Victoria James.

I’m looking forward to learning from people who understand the challenges and opportunities of building a restaurant business, while connecting with operators who bring different experiences and perspectives to the table.

Why does leadership training matter for restaurant owners?

Many of us learned this business on the job. According to the National Restaurant Association, about 80% of restaurant owners and managers started in entry-level positions. That was my path, too.

I built MIX Bricktown, The LOCKEROOM Sports Lounge, Sandbox and Me & You through long nights, hard lessons and a commitment to customer service.

Experience is a great teacher, but it isn’t the only one. Formal training can give operators a framework for strengthening the skills that keep a business moving: building effective teams, managing cash flow, developing operational systems and creating guest experiences people remember.

And the learning doesn’t stop once a business becomes established. As a hospitality group grows, leadership becomes more complex. What works for one concept may not work for four. Continuing to learn helps me evaluate how we operate, identify opportunities to improve and prepare for what’s next.

What will Wright Hospitality gain from the Restaurant Academy?

I’m going in with three goals.

First, I want to sharpen the systems that allow four distinct concepts to operate at a consistently high standard while maintaining their individual identities.

Second, I want to pursue certifications that support professional development and strengthen our team’s knowledge of the hospitality industry.

Third, I want to build relationships with operators across the country who are navigating similar challenges in their own markets. Learning how other people approach staffing, operations, customer experience and growth can introduce perspectives I might not encounter working only within my own businesses.

I have always believed that education helps entrepreneurs move beyond simply keeping a business open. That is true in year one and in year 15. Growth doesn’t stop once you scale; it changes shape.

What comes next for Wright Hospitality?

The cohort continues through March 2027, and I’m looking forward to applying what I learn to the next chapter of Wright Hospitality.

Detroit’s hospitality scene continues to evolve, and independent operators have an opportunity to help shape what comes next. That requires more than vision. It requires preparation, strong operations, meaningful relationships and a willingness to keep learning.

To every restaurant owner reading this: Keep applying, keep learning and keep showing up. Invest in your own development, even when you’re busy running the business. The room you want to be in may be closer than you think.
